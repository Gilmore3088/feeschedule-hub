In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$32 vs $12your NSF fee vs peer median

Your NSF fee is an extreme outlier.At $32.00, your returned-item fee is 2.7x the median of 35 peer banks — the 89th percentile. A quarter of your peers now charge $5 or less, reflecting the industry-wide move away from NSF fees under regulatory scrutiny. This is the single most visible line on your fee schedule.

P94incoming domestic wire percentile

You charge a quiet premium on exception activity.Incoming domestic wires ($20 vs a $15 median) sit at the 94th percentile, and overdraft-protection transfers run double the peer median ($10 vs $5). Individually small, together these place your penalty-and-exception fee stack in the top quartile of your cohort.

-10%stop payment vs peer median

You are below market on service fees — and nobody knows.Stop payments ($27 vs $30 median, 27th percentile) and card replacement ($7.50 vs $10.00 median) are priced below most peers. That is either unclaimed margin or an untold customer-friendly story; today it is neither, because it is invisible.

Net position: Bank of the Pacific prices core account services at market (monthly maintenance and overdraft are within a dollar of the peer median) while carrying a distinctly top-quartile posture on penalty and exception fees — led by an NSF fee that qualifies as an extreme statistical outlier against 35 comparable banks. The exposure is asymmetrical: the outlier fees invite attention, while the below-market service fees earn no credit. The three findings above are the agenda for a one-hour pricing conversation.

03 · What stands outBank of the Pacific
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

NSF / returned item — $32.00

Peer median $12.00 · P25 $5.00 · 89th percentile · n=35 · flagged: extreme outlier (>2x median)

The peer distribution tells the story: a quarter of comparable banks now charge $5 or less, and the median has fallen to $12 as institutions retire NSF fees ahead of regulatory and reputational pressure. A $32 NSF fee is increasingly conspicuous — in examiner conversations, in local-press fee surveys, and on comparison sites. Whether the right answer is a cut, a cap, or a documented rationale, this line item deserves a decision rather than a default.

Incoming domestic wire — $20.00

Peer median $15.00 · P75 $15.00 · 94th percentile · n=17

Charging above the 75th percentile to RECEIVE money is a friction fee on your most attractive customers — businesses and high-balance households move wires. At $5 over median it earns little revenue but sits at the very top of the cohort.

OD protection transfer — $10.00

Peer median $5.00 · 83rd percentile · n=23

Customers who enroll in overdraft protection are doing exactly what institutions ask of them; charging double the peer median for the transfer undercuts the message. This fee is also increasingly waived outright among competitors.

Service fees — priced below market, marketed nowhere

Stop payment $27 vs $30 median (P27) · Card replacement $7.50 vs $10.00 median (P28)

Two consumer-facing service fees run meaningfully below your cohort. If deliberate, they belong in your marketing: 'lower service fees than most banks in your market' is a claim your competitors' own published schedules substantiate. If not deliberate, they are margin available without touching a single headline fee.

06 · Market contextBank of the Pacific
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from NSF: large banks have largely eliminated returned-item fees, and the pressure has migrated down-market — visible in your own cohort, where the NSF median ($12) now sits at less than half your posted fee and a quarter of peers charge $5 or less. Second, deposit competition has turned fee schedules into marketing documents: published fees are public, scraped, and compared, which converts every outlier line into a small reputational bet.

Community-scale institutions ($1B–$10B) occupy the exposed middle: large enough to be included in comparisons, without the brand gravity that lets money-center banks charge premiums quietly. In that position, the highest-leverage moves are usually not across-the-board cuts but surgical ones — retiring or capping the one or two conspicuous outliers while actively advertising the fees already priced below market.

Methodology & data provenance

Fee data is drawn exclusively from institutions' published fee schedules — public documents — collected and verified through the Bank Fee Index agentic pipeline (discovery, extraction, independent verification, publication). Peer cohort: Community banks, $1B–$10B — 86 institutions with published data. Percentiles are computed within-cohort per fee category; a category is flagged an outlier when it exceeds 2× the peer median (extreme) or P75 + 1.5×IQR (statistical). Data pulled Aug 16, 2026. Figures reflect the lowest published consumer-facing amount per category and may not capture account-specific waivers.

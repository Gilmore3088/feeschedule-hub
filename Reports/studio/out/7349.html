$0two fees fully waived

You waive fees your peers still charge.Non-network ATM transactions ($0 against a $1.00 peer median) and minimum-balance charges ($0 against a $4.00 median) are waived outright on your schedule. These are exactly the small, frequent frictions members notice — and exactly the claims that comparison-minded members verify against published schedules. $5 vs $25card replacement vs peer median

Your service-fee stack runs well below market.Card replacement ($5.00 vs a $25.00 median, 31st percentile), overdraft-protection transfers ($5.00 vs a $20.00 median), and stop payments ($10.00 vs $15.00, 33rd percentile) all price under most comparable credit unions. Combined with the waived fees above, this is a coherent member-value posture — undermined only by the NSF outlier, and unclaimed as a story.

Net position: Dfcu Financial runs one of the more member-friendly published schedules in its cohort — two fees waived outright, a service-fee stack below market — with a single glaring exception: a $32 NSF fee that is the highest among the five comparable large credit unions with published data and more than double their median. One caveat on the data: peer samples in this cohort are thin (four to thirteen institutions for most categories), so percentiles should be read as directional. The strategic question is coherence: either the NSF fee comes into line with the rest of the schedule, or the member-value story stays untellable because its biggest counterexample sits on page one of the fee schedule.

03 · What stands outDfcu Financial Federal Credit Union
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

NSF / returned item — $32.00

Peer median $15.00 · P25 $7.00 · 100th percentile · n=5 · flagged: extreme outlier (>2x median)

The peer set is small, but its shape is unambiguous: the median is $15, a quarter of the set charges $7 or less, and one comparable credit union has retired the fee altogether. At $32 you hold the top of the distribution during an industry-wide retreat from NSF fees. For an institution whose other fees signal member value, this line is the outlier in both senses — statistical and strategic. It warrants a decision: cut, cap, or a documented rationale.

Waived fees — $0 where peers charge

ATM non-network $0.00 vs $1.00 median · Minimum balance $0.00 vs $4.00 median

Two fees your peers still charge, you have eliminated. These waivers are verifiable in your published schedule and directly comparable against peers' — which makes them low-risk marketing claims with real substance. A member choosing between large credit unions can confirm the difference in five minutes; today, nothing invites them to look.

Service fees — priced below market, marketed nowhere

Card replacement $5.00 vs $25.00 median (P31) · OD protection transfer $5.00 vs $20.00 median · Stop payment $10.00 vs $15.00 median (P33)

Three consumer-facing service fees run at a fraction of the cohort median. If deliberate, they complete a member-value narrative that spans the whole schedule and deserves to be told. If not deliberate, they are margin available without touching a headline fee. The one move to avoid is the current default: below market, and invisible.

06 · Market contextDfcu Financial Federal Credit Union
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from NSF: the largest institutions have largely eliminated returned-item fees, and the pressure has migrated across the industry — visible even in your small peer set, where the median has fallen to $15 and one comparable credit union charges nothing. Second, published fee schedules have become marketing documents: public, scraped, and compared, which converts every outlier line into a reputational bet and every waiver into a verifiable claim.

Large community credit unions occupy the exposed middle of that landscape: big enough to appear in every comparison and press survey, without the brand gravity that lets money-center banks price premiums quietly. What credit unions at your scale do have is the member-ownership story — and your schedule already substantiates it almost everywhere. In that position the highest-leverage move is rarely an across-the-board cut; it is resolving the single conspicuous outlier so the rest of the schedule can be advertised without an asterisk.

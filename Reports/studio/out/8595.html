01 · Executive summaryUnion Square Credit Union
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$30 vs $24.50overdraft fee vs peer median

Your overdraft fee ties for highest in the cohort.At $30.00, your overdraft fee sits at the 100th percentile of 6 comparable credit unions — matching the cohort maximum, $5.50 over the median. The cohort is small, so treat the percentile with care, but the direction is unambiguous: on the one fee regulators and members watch most, you are at the top of your peer group.

$5 vs $35card replacement vs peer median

You charge a fraction of what peers charge to replace a card.Card replacement runs $5.00 against a $35.00 median across 23 comparable credit unions — the 17th percentile, $30 below the middle of the pack. This is a genuinely member-friendly price on a high-emotion service moment (cards are usually replaced after loss or fraud), and today it earns you no credit because nobody knows.

P20deposited item return percentile

You are priced at or below market almost everywhere else.Deposited-item returns ($15 vs a $32 median, 20th percentile), paper statements ($1 vs $3), and overdraft-protection transfers ($2 vs a $2 median) all sit at or below peers. Outside of overdraft, this schedule reads as one of the most member-friendly in the cohort — a story worth telling deliberately.

Net position: Union Square Credit Union runs one of the most member-friendly published schedules in its cohort — card replacement at a seventh of the peer median, deposited-item returns at less than half, paper statements at a third — with a single exception: an overdraft fee that ties for the highest among 6 comparable credit unions. That is an unusual and fixable asymmetry. The overdraft line is the one members and examiners will notice; everything else is a marketing asset going unused. One decision and one communications plan cover the whole agenda.

03 · What stands outUnion Square Credit Union
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

Overdraft — $30.00

Peer median $24.50 · peer max $30.00 · 100th percentile · n=6

You share the top of the cohort on the single most scrutinized fee in consumer finance. The peer group here is small (6 credit unions), which argues for caution on the percentile — but not on the exposure: overdraft is the fee that appears in examiner conversations and member complaints, and yours is $5.50 over the median while the rest of your schedule undercuts peers. A modest cut would make the whole schedule consistent with the member-friendly posture you already have everywhere else.

Card replacement — $5.00 vs a $35.00 median

Peer median $35.00 · 17th percentile · n=23

This is the largest below-market gap on your schedule — $30 under the median, against the broadest peer sample in your pack. Members compare exactly this kind of fee when a card is lost or compromised. 'We replace your card for $5; most comparable credit unions charge $35' is a claim your peers' own published schedules substantiate, and it costs nothing to make.

The rest of the member-service stack — below market, unmarketed

Deposited item return $15 vs $32 median (P20) · Paper statement $1 vs $3 (P40) · OD protection transfer $2 vs $2 median

Beyond card replacement, the service fees members actually encounter run at or below the cohort across the board. If this is deliberate strategy, it belongs in member communications and new-member onboarding; if it is inertia, it is at least worth confirming as policy before a future repricing quietly erodes it.

06 · Market contextUnion Square Credit Union
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the industry-wide retreat from penalty pricing: NSF and overdraft fees have been falling across consumer finance under regulatory and reputational pressure, and overdraft in particular has become the line examiners and journalists check first. Second, published fee schedules have become marketing documents — public, scraped, and compared — which turns every outlier line into a small reputational bet and every below-market line into an unclaimed asset.

Mid-sized credit unions sit in an exposed middle: large enough to appear in fee comparisons alongside banks and larger credit unions, without the marketing budgets that let bigger institutions shape the narrative. For an institution whose schedule already undercuts comparable credit unions almost everywhere, the highest-leverage moves are narrow — bring the one top-of-cohort fee back toward the pack, then make the member-friendly pricing you already practice a named, advertised policy rather than an accident of history.

Methodology & data provenance

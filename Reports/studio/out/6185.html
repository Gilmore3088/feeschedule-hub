01 · Executive summaryPeoples Federal Credit Union
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$10 vs $25card replacement vs peer median

You are meaningfully below market where members feel it most.Card replacement runs $10 against a $25 median across 94 comparable credit unions — the 38th percentile and a $15 gap on the broadest peer sample in your pack. International wires ($25 vs $40, 17th percentile) and paper statements ($1 vs $2.75, 25th percentile) tell the same story: on member-facing services, you undercut the cohort and take no credit for it.

$25 vs $20stop payment vs peer median

A small premium sits on your exception fees.Stop payments ($25 vs a $20 median, 68th percentile of 66 peers), deposited-item returns ($25 vs $20, 68th percentile), and cashier's checks ($5 vs $4, 90th percentile) each run modestly above market. Individually minor — $1 to $5 per line — but together they are the only above-median cluster on your schedule, and the easiest one to tidy.

4 gapscategories missing from published data

Overdraft is invisible to comparison — that cuts both ways.Overdraft, monthly maintenance, non-network ATM, and incoming domestic wire fees did not appear in your published schedule data. Your NSF fee, which is published, sits exactly at the $25 peer median. If your unpublished fees are similarly at-market, publishing them is free transparency; if not, better to know before a member or examiner finds out first.

Net position: Peoples Federal Credit Union is priced at or below market almost everywhere — NSF exactly at the $25 median of 66 comparable credit unions, card replacement at $15 under the median, international wires $15 under, paper statements a third of the going rate. The exceptions are small: a $5 premium on stop payments and deposited-item returns, and a dollar on cashier's checks. The larger issue is visibility — four benchmark categories, including overdraft, are missing from the published data, and the genuinely member-friendly pricing that is published goes unadvertised. This is a schedule with little to fix and a lot to say.

Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

A below-market service stack, marketed nowhere

Card replacement $10 vs $25 median (P38, n=94) · Intl wire $25 vs $40 (P17) · Paper statement $1 vs $2.75 (P25) · OD protection transfer $3 vs $5

Across the fees members actually encounter, you undercut comparable credit unions — in card replacement's case against 94 peers, the strongest sample in this report. 'Lower everyday fees than most credit unions' is a claim your peers' own published schedules substantiate. Today it appears in no member communication, which means it earns you nothing.

Exception fees — a quiet $5 premium

Stop payment $25 vs $20 (P68, n=66) · Deposited item return $25 vs $20 (P68) · Cashier's check $5 vs $4 (P90)

Three exception and service lines run modestly above the cohort. None is an outlier, and none is worth a headline — but they are the only lines preventing a clean 'at or below market everywhere' claim. Trimming them, or documenting why they stay, closes the gap between your pricing and your story.

Unpublished fees — overdraft, maintenance, ATM, incoming wire

4 of 15 benchmark categories flagged as data gaps · overdraft peer median $25.00 · maintenance peer median $3.50

The most consequential gap is overdraft — the fee members, journalists, and examiners check first. Your published NSF fee sits exactly at the peer median, which suggests your unpublished lines may be similarly reasonable; publishing them would convert an information gap into a transparency point and complete an otherwise strong member-friendly schedule.

06 · Market contextPeoples Federal Credit Union
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the industry-wide retreat from penalty pricing: NSF and overdraft fees have fallen across consumer finance under regulatory and reputational pressure, and the institutions that moved early have made fee schedules part of their pitch. Second, published fee schedules have become marketing documents — public, scraped, and compared — which turns every below-market line into an unclaimed asset and every unpublished line into an open question.

Small community credit unions compete on relationships, and a fee schedule like this one is relationship pricing in writing: card replacement at less than half the going rate, statements at a third, wires well under market. For an institution serving its members this well on price, the work is not repricing — it is closing the four publication gaps, deciding the handful of $5 premiums deliberately, and then saying out loud, in branches and onboarding materials, what the numbers already show.

Methodology & data provenance

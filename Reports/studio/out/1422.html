01 · Executive summaryPB&T Bank
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$25 vs $12deposited item return vs median

Your deposited item return fee is an extreme outlier.At $25.00 — more than double the $12.00 median of 15 peer banks and tied for the cohort maximum at the 100th percentile — this fee is flagged as an extreme outlier. It also lands on the wrong customer: a deposited item return charges your customer for someone else's bad check. That combination of top-of-market price and no-fault incidence makes it the most defensible line to cut on the schedule.

$29 vs $15your NSF fee vs peer median

Your NSF fee is nearly double the peer median.Across 34 comparable banks the NSF median has fallen to $15, and a quarter of peers now charge $6.25 or less. Your $29 is close to the cohort's top cluster (75th percentile: $30). As the most examined fee in the industry continues its retreat, the dollar gap — not the percentile — is the number to manage: you charge nearly twice what the typical peer does.

$20 vs $40international wire vs peer median

You charge half the market rate on international wires — quietly.At $20 against a $40 median (11th percentile, 9 peers), international wires are your largest below-market line, and everything else on the schedule sits within a dollar or two of the median — overdraft $29 vs $30, stop payment $29 vs $30, card replacement exactly at $10. The schedule is at-market with two penalty exceptions above and one service exception below.

Net position: PB&T Bank prices its core schedule at market — overdraft, stop payment, card replacement, cashier's checks, and domestic wires all sit within a dollar or two of the medians of its 76-bank cohort. The exposure is concentrated in two penalty lines: a deposited item return fee flagged as an extreme outlier at the cohort maximum, and an NSF fee at nearly double the peer median. On the other side of the ledger, international wires run at half the market rate with no marketing attached, and the monthly maintenance fee is absent from the published schedule entirely. The three findings above are the agenda for a one-hour pricing conversation.

03 · What stands outPB&T Bank
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

Deposited item return — $25.00

Peer median $12.00 · P75 $16.50 · 100th percentile · n=15 · flagged: extreme outlier (>2x median)

This is the cohort maximum on a fee charged to the party who did nothing wrong — your customer deposited a check that someone else failed to cover. Regulators have singled out exactly this fee category in junk-fee guidance for that reason. At more than double the median, it earns modest revenue for outsized examiner and reputational exposure; it deserves a deliberate decision, and the defensible answers are a cut or a documented rationale.

NSF / returned item — $29.00

Peer median $15.00 · P25 $6.25 · 65th percentile · n=34

The cohort has split: a cluster of peers still charges $30, but the median has fallen to $15 and a quarter of the market is at $6.25 or under. The direction of travel is one way. Deciding now where PB&T lands — follow the median, hold with the top cluster, or stage a reduction — keeps this a pricing choice rather than a reaction to the next round of scrutiny.

Monthly maintenance — not found on published schedule

Peer median $12.00 · n=15 · no published value (data gap)

Fifteen peers publish a maintenance fee; yours could not be verified. Unpublished fees read as hidden fees to comparison tools and cautious shoppers, whatever the actual price. If accounts are genuinely free, that is a marketing claim going unmade; if not, the schedule should say what the number is.

International wire — $20.00 vs a $40.00 median

Peer median $40.00 · P25 $35.00 · 11th percentile · n=9

You charge half the market rate on a fee that matters to exactly the business and internationally connected customers most banks court hardest. If deliberate, it belongs in commercial and relationship-banking materials — peers' own published schedules substantiate the claim. If legacy pricing, it is margin available without touching any penalty line.

06 · Market contextPB&T Bank
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from NSF and penalty fees: large banks have largely eliminated returned-item charges, and the pressure has migrated down-market — visible in your own cohort, where the NSF median has fallen to $15 and a quarter of peers charge $6.25 or less. Deposit-related penalty fees, including deposited item returns, have drawn the same junk-fee scrutiny. Second, published fee schedules have become public documents: scraped, compared, and surfaced automatically, which converts every outlier line into a small reputational bet and every unpublished line into an assumed one.

Mid-sized community banks occupy the exposed middle: large enough to be swept into fee comparisons and examiner attention, without the brand gravity that lets money-center banks carry premiums quietly. In that position the highest-leverage moves are surgical, not sweeping — PB&T's core schedule is already at market, so the work is retiring or justifying the two conspicuous penalty outliers, publishing the missing maintenance line, and taking public credit for the international wire pricing that already undercuts the market by half.

Methodology & data provenance

Fee data is drawn exclusively from institutions' published fee schedules — public documents — collected and verified through the Bank Fee Index agentic pipeline (discovery, extraction, independent verification, publication). Peer cohort: Community banks, $300M–$1B — 76 institutions with published data. Percentiles are computed within-cohort per fee category; a category is flagged an outlier when it exceeds 2× the peer median (extreme) or P75 + 1.5×IQR (statistical). Data pulled Aug 16, 2026. Figures reflect the lowest published consumer-facing amount per category and may not capture account-specific waivers.

01 · Executive summaryBay Cities Credit Union
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$0 vs $5OD protection transfer vs median

You waive two fees most peers charge — silently.Overdraft-protection transfers are free against a $5.00 peer median (13th percentile, 24 publishing peers), and monthly maintenance is free against a $3.50 median (21st percentile, 33 peers). Waived fees that peers charge are the strongest possible comparison claim — and right now they generate zero marketing value because nobody is told about them.

$5 vs $25card replacement vs peer median

Your service fees run well below market across the board.Card replacement ($5 vs a $25 median across 94 peers, 24th percentile), paper statements ($1 vs $2.75, 25th percentile), and international wires ($30 vs $40, 28th percentile) all price in the bottom third of the cohort. Combined with the waived fees, Bay Cities has one of the most member-friendly published schedules in its comparison set.

2 feesmissing from published schedule

Your overdraft fee is invisible — and that now costs you.No overdraft fee and no incoming domestic wire fee could be verified from your published schedule. Overdraft is the single most scrutinized fee in the industry; when it cannot be found, comparison tools and cautious prospective members assume the worst rather than the best. For an institution priced this favorably everywhere else, an unpublished overdraft line surrenders the exact comparison you would win.

Net position: Bay Cities Credit Union prices at the median on the penalty core (NSF $25 and stop payment $20 both sit exactly at the peer median of a 182-credit-union cohort) and at or below market everywhere else — two fees waived outright, card replacement at a fifth of the median, statements and international wires in the bottom third. There is no genuine overpricing finding on this schedule. The exposure is informational, not financial: the overdraft and incoming wire fees are absent from the published schedule, leaving the most examined line in banking invisible. The three findings above are the agenda for a one-hour pricing conversation.

03 · What stands outBay Cities Credit Union
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

Waived fees — free where peers charge

Monthly maintenance $0 vs $3.50 median (P21, n=33) · OD protection transfer $0 vs $5.00 median (P13, n=24)

Free maintenance and free overdraft-protection transfers are claims your competitors' own published schedules substantiate. These are precisely the lines prospective members compare when choosing an institution, and 'we don't charge for that' is the shortest marketing sentence in banking. It belongs on the website, in branch materials, and in member acquisition messaging.

Service fees — bottom third of the cohort

Card replacement $5 vs $25 median (P24, n=94) · Paper statement $1 vs $2.75 (P25) · Intl wire $30 vs $40 (P28)

Against the largest and most reliable comparison sets in this analysis, Bay Cities undercuts the market on everyday service fees. If deliberate, this is a differentiation story; if legacy, it is margin available without touching a single penalty line. Either way it deserves a decision — and if the answer is 'keep it,' it deserves an audience.

Overdraft and incoming wire — not found on published schedule

Overdraft: peer median $25.00, n=27, no published value · Incoming domestic wire: peer median $10.00, n=13, no published value

Unpublished fees are read as hidden fees, whatever the actual price. In an era of junk-fee scrutiny and automated schedule comparison, transparency itself has become a competitive attribute — and an institution with this favorable a pricing profile has every incentive to publish. If the overdraft fee is at or below the $25 cohort median, disclosure is pure upside; if it is above, that is a pricing conversation worth having before an examiner or a journalist has it for you.

06 · Market contextBay Cities Credit Union
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from penalty fees: NSF and overdraft charges are being cut, capped, or eliminated under regulatory and reputational pressure, and your own cohort reflects it — the NSF median across 182 comparable credit unions has settled at $25, exactly where you price. Second, published fee schedules have become public documents: scraped, compared, and surfaced to prospective members automatically. That cuts both ways — every outlier is exposed, but every below-market line and every waived fee is independently verifiable by anyone who looks.

Small community credit unions win on relationships and trust, and a fee schedule is one of the few places that promise is testable in writing. Bay Cities' schedule already passes the test almost everywhere — waived maintenance, waived protection transfers, bottom-third service fees. The two highest-leverage moves are therefore unusual ones: publish the missing overdraft and wire lines so the schedule's transparency matches its pricing, and then market the member-friendly profile the data already proves.

Methodology & data provenance

Fee data is drawn exclusively from institutions' published fee schedules — public documents — collected and verified through the Bank Fee Index agentic pipeline (discovery, extraction, independent verification, publication). Peer cohort: Community credit unions, under $300M — 182 institutions with published data. Percentiles are computed within-cohort per fee category; a category is flagged an outlier when it exceeds 2× the peer median (extreme) or P75 + 1.5×IQR (statistical). Data pulled Aug 16, 2026. Figures reflect the lowest published consumer-facing amount per category and may not capture account-specific waivers.

01 · Executive summaryCoopers Cave Federal Credit Union
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$43 vs $20stop payment vs peer median

Your stop-payment fee is an extreme outlier.At $43.00, your stop-payment fee is more than double the $20 median of 66 comparable credit unions — the 98th percentile, flagged as an extreme outlier. Three quarters of your peers charge $30 or less. This is a service fee members request in moments of stress, which makes an outlier price here disproportionately visible.

P100deposited-item return percentile

Two more fees sit at the very top of the cohort.Deposited-item returns run $40.00 against a $20.00 median — the highest value among 25 peers — and paper statements run $5.00 against a $2.75 median, at the top of 28 peers. Add an NSF fee of $30 versus a $25 median (83rd percentile), and your penalty-and-exception stack occupies the cohort's ceiling across multiple lines.

$3 vs $25card replacement vs peer median

Your everyday fees are genuinely member-friendly — and unknown.Card replacement ($3.00 vs a $25.00 median, 11th percentile across 94 peers), monthly maintenance ($1.00 vs $3.50, 39th percentile), and cashier's checks ($2.00 vs $4.00, 31st percentile) all price well below comparable credit unions. The schedule's generous half is real; it simply earns nothing today because nobody has been told about it.

Net position: Coopers Cave runs a barbell schedule. Exception and penalty fees sit at or near the top of the cohort — a stop-payment fee flagged as an extreme outlier, deposited-item returns and paper statements at the cohort maximum, NSF above a falling median — while everyday service fees like card replacement and monthly maintenance price near the bottom. Three categories (overdraft, overdraft-protection transfer, incoming domestic wire) are absent from the published schedule. The barbell is the risk: members and reviewers notice the ceiling fees, and the generous floor earns no offsetting credit. The three findings above are the agenda for a one-hour pricing conversation.

03 · What stands outCoopers Cave Federal Credit Union
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

Stop payment — $43.00

Peer median $20.00 · P75 $30.00 · 98th percentile · n=66 · flagged: extreme outlier (>2x median)

At more than double the peer median, this is the single most conspicuous line on your schedule. Stop payments are requested by members already dealing with a problem — a lost check, a disputed payment — so the fee lands at a moment of maximum sensitivity. Whether the answer is a cut toward the $20 center or a documented rationale, this fee should be a decision rather than an inheritance.

Deposited item return — $40.00

Peer median $20.00 · peer max $40.00 · 100th percentile · n=25

You hold the cohort ceiling on a fee that charges members because someone else's check failed — a charge members consistently experience as unfair, since the failure is not theirs. Fees that penalize members for third-party behavior are exactly the category regulators and comparison surveys highlight first. At double the median, this line pairs with the stop-payment outlier to define your schedule's exposure.

NSF / returned item — $30.00

Peer median $25.00 · 83rd percentile · n=66

The industry-wide retreat from NSF fees has reached small credit unions: your cohort's median is $25 and the low end has already reached zero. A $30 fee is not yet an outlier, but it sits on the wrong side of a falling trend line and compounds the top-of-cohort posture established by your other exception fees.

Everyday fees — near the bottom of the cohort, marketed nowhere

Card replacement $3.00 vs $25.00 median (P11) · Monthly maintenance $1.00 vs $3.50 median (P39) · Cashier's check $2.00 vs $4.00 median (P31)

Three routine member fees price at a fraction of the cohort. If deliberate, they are the foundation of a member-value story your peers' own published schedules substantiate — one that could offset the exception-fee optics above. If not deliberate, they are margin available without touching a headline fee. Below market and invisible is the one position that earns nothing.

06 · Market contextCoopers Cave Federal Credit Union
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from NSF and penalty pricing generally: the largest institutions have eliminated returned-item fees, regulatory attention to so-called junk fees has intensified, and the pressure now reaches small credit unions — visible in your own cohort, where a quarter of peers price NSF at $20 or less. Second, published fee schedules have become public documents in practice: scraped, compared, and surfaced in searches, which converts every top-of-cohort line into a small reputational bet.

Small community credit unions compete on relationship and trust, which makes fee coherence unusually valuable at your scale — members forgive a fee they understand and resent one that feels arbitrary, and a $43 stop payment next to a $1 maintenance fee reads as arbitrary. The highest-leverage moves here are surgical: bring the two or three ceiling fees toward the cohort center, publish the categories currently missing from the schedule, and start telling the genuinely member-friendly story the bottom half of your schedule already contains.

In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$60 vs $35international wire vs peer median

Your international wire fee tops the entire cohort.At $60.00, your outgoing international wire is the highest of 10 peer banks — the 100th percentile, $25 over the $35 median and above the cohort's 75th percentile of $47.50. Paired with a $30 outgoing domestic wire (93rd percentile vs a $20 median), customers moving money out pay a consistent top-of-market premium.

2.5xOD protection transfer vs peer median

You charge an extreme premium on overdraft protection.Your $12.50 overdraft-protection transfer fee is a flagged extreme outlier — two and a half times the $5.00 median of 23 peers, at the 87th percentile. Customers who enroll in overdraft protection are doing exactly what banks ask of them; charging 2.5x the market rate for the transfer undercuts the message, and your $20 NSF fee (69th percentile vs a $12 median) compounds the penalty-fee posture.

5 of 15core fee categories unpublished

A third of your fee schedule is invisible to comparison.Overdraft, monthly maintenance, paper statement, card replacement, and cashier's check fees do not appear in your published schedule data — five of the fifteen categories we benchmark. In a market where published fees are scraped and compared, unpublished fees read as either a transparency gap or a missed chance to compete on lines where you may be well priced.

Net position: Burke & Herbert prices money movement and penalty exceptions at the top of its cohort — the highest international wire fee of 10 peers, a 93rd-percentile domestic outgoing wire, an overdraft-protection transfer flagged as an extreme outlier at 2.5x the median, and an NSF fee $8 over the market. Meanwhile, five core categories are absent from the published schedule entirely, so the fees customers can see skew toward the expensive ones. The agenda is twofold: decide which of the outlier fees deserve their premium, and close the disclosure gap so the schedule presents the whole picture rather than its worst lines.

03 · What stands outBurke & Herbert Bank & Trust Company
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

International wire (outgoing) — $60.00

Peer median $35.00 · P75 $47.50 · 100th percentile · n=10

No peer charges more. At $25 over the median, this is the single largest dollar gap on your schedule, and it lands on internationally connected households and businesses — customers with above-average balances and above-average alternatives, including fintech remittance providers that publish their pricing aggressively. This line deserves either a competitive rationale or a cut.

OD protection transfer — $12.50

Peer median $5.00 · 87th percentile · n=23 · flagged: extreme outlier (>2x median)

This fee is charged to customers who opted into the responsible behavior banks promote. At 2.5x the peer median — and with a growing share of competitors waiving it outright — it is the kind of line item that features in junk-fee coverage. The revenue is modest; the framing risk is not.

Outgoing domestic wire — $30.00

Peer median $20.00 · P75 $25.00 · 93rd percentile · n=15

Fifty percent over the median and above the 75th percentile of the cohort. Combined with the international wire fee, both outbound money-movement lines price at the top of the market — a consistent premium on business and high-balance customers, the segment most likely to compare.

Five unpublished categories — control the comparison

No published data: overdraft · monthly maintenance · paper statement · card replacement · cashier's check

Comparison sites and fee surveys fill gaps with assumptions rarely favorable to the institution. Publishing complete fee information is a low-cost transparency win — and if any of these five fees are priced at or below market, disclosure converts them from invisible to advantageous. Right now the visible schedule leads with your most expensive lines.

06 · Market contextBurke & Herbert Bank & Trust Company
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from penalty fees: large banks have largely eliminated NSF fees and softened overdraft programs, and the pressure has migrated down-market — visible in your own cohort, where the NSF median ($12) sits $8 below your posted fee and overdraft-protection transfers are increasingly waived. Second, deposit competition has turned fee schedules into marketing documents: published fees are public, scraped, and compared, which converts every outlier line into a small reputational bet and every unpublished line into a gap someone else fills.

Community-scale institutions ($1B–$10B) occupy the exposed middle: large enough to be included in comparisons, without the brand gravity that lets money-center banks charge premiums quietly. In that position, the highest-leverage moves are usually surgical — bringing the one or two top-of-cohort fees back toward the market, retiring the extreme outlier on overdraft protection, and completing the published schedule so the comparison customers inevitably run is one you have chosen to shape.

01 · Executive summaryGateway Bank
In brief

Three things your fee schedule says about you

$30 vs $15NSF fee vs peer median

Your NSF fee is double the peer median.At $30.00, your NSF fee is twice the $15 median of 34 comparable banks — the 82nd percentile. A quarter of those peers now charge $6.25 or less, reflecting the industry-wide move away from returned-item fees under regulatory scrutiny. This is the most visible penalty fee on your schedule, and the market beneath it is falling.

P80monthly maintenance percentile

Core account fees carry a quiet premium.Monthly maintenance runs $17.00 against a $12.00 median — the 80th percentile of 15 peers, above the cohort's 75th-percentile mark of $16. Incoming domestic wires add to the pattern at $15.00 versus a $12.50 median (83rd percentile). Neither gap is dramatic alone, but together they price your everyday relationship fees in the top quintile of the cohort.

$0.08 vs $12deposited item return vs median

You nearly give one fee away — and hide two others.Your deposited-item return fee is eight cents against a $12.00 peer median — the 7th percentile, effectively a waiver that earns you no credit because it is nowhere promoted. Meanwhile two consumer-facing categories, card replacement and paper statements, are absent from your published schedule entirely. Customers reading your schedule see the premiums but not the generosity.

Net position: Gateway Bank prices its most visible fees — NSF and monthly maintenance — in the top quintile of 76 comparable mid-size banks, while quietly holding a near-zero deposited-item return fee that earns no marketing credit and leaving two categories unpublished altogether. Transaction fees are otherwise near market: outgoing domestic wires match the pattern of peers, international wires sit below the median, and overdraft matches the $30 peer median. The exposure is concentrated in exactly the lines customers and examiners look at first. The three findings above are the agenda for a one-hour pricing conversation.

03 · What stands outGateway Bank
Divergences

Where you diverge from your market

NSF / returned item — $30.00

Peer median $15.00 · P25 $6.25 · 82nd percentile · n=34

The peer distribution tells the story: the median has fallen to $15 and a quarter of comparable banks charge $6.25 or less as institutions retire NSF fees ahead of regulatory and reputational pressure. A fee at double the median is increasingly conspicuous — in examiner conversations, in local fee surveys, and on comparison sites. Whether the right answer is a cut, a cap, or a documented rationale, this line deserves a decision rather than a default.

Monthly maintenance — $17.00

Peer median $12.00 · P75 $16.00 · 80th percentile · n=15

This is the fee every account holder sees every month, and yours sits above the cohort's 75th percentile. A $5-over-median maintenance fee is the kind of recurring gap that drives silent attrition rather than complaints — customers do not call about it; they leave over it. If waiver thresholds offset it in practice, that logic should be visible; if not, the price point warrants review.

Deposited item return — $0.08

Peer median $12.00 · 7th percentile · n=15

At eight cents you have effectively waived a fee peers charge $12 for — and one that customers experience as especially unfair elsewhere, since it punishes them for someone else's bad check. This is a ready-made fairness story: 'we do not charge you when a deposited check bounces' is a claim your competitors' published schedules make credible. Today it is invisible.

Unpublished categories — card replacement, paper statement

Both absent from published schedule · flagged: data gap · peer medians $10.00 and $3.58

Two everyday consumer fees do not appear in your published schedule. In a market where fee schedules are scraped and compared, an unpublished fee reads either as free or as hidden — and only one of those interpretations helps you. Publishing them costs nothing if they are at or below the peer medians, and closes a transparency gap examiners and comparison sites increasingly notice.

06 · Market contextGateway Bank
The bigger picture

What is moving in your market

Two currents define the fee landscape your schedule competes in. First, the retreat from NSF: large banks have largely eliminated returned-item fees, and the pressure has migrated down-market — visible in your own cohort, where the NSF median ($15) is half your posted fee and a quarter of peers charge $6.25 or less. Second, deposit competition has turned fee schedules into marketing documents: published fees are public, scraped, and compared, which converts every outlier line into a small reputational bet and every unpublished line into an open question.

Mid-size community banks occupy the exposed middle: large enough to be included in comparisons, without the brand gravity that lets money-center banks charge premiums quietly. In that position, the highest-leverage moves are usually not across-the-board cuts but surgical ones — bringing the one or two conspicuous fees toward the cohort center, publishing the missing lines, and actively advertising the fee you have already all but waived.

Methodology & data provenance

Fee data is drawn exclusively from institutions' published fee schedules — public documents — collected and verified through the Bank Fee Index agentic pipeline (discovery, extraction, independent verification, publication). Peer cohort: Community banks, $300M–$1B — 76 institutions with published data. Percentiles are computed within-cohort per fee category; a category is flagged an outlier when it exceeds 2× the peer median (extreme) or P75 + 1.5×IQR (statistical). Data pulled Aug 16, 2026. Figures reflect the lowest published consumer-facing amount per category and may not capture account-specific waivers.
